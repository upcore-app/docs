upcore watches your services, records every check, and publishes what it finds on
a status page you can hand to your users.

It is a Nuxt 4 application with a Nitro server, shipped as a single container. It
stores everything in SQLite by default — one file, no database server — and talks
to PostgreSQL just as well when a deployment outgrows that.

## Read a heartbeat

Every check writes one heartbeat. It has three states, and the third one is the
reason upcore does not wake you up for a blip:

| State | Value | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| up | `1` | The check passed. |
| down | `0` | The check failed, and it has failed `failureThreshold` times in a row. |
| pending | `2` | The check failed, but not often enough yet. Recorded, never announced. |

A monitor with `failureThreshold: 3` and a 60-second interval therefore takes
three minutes of continuous failure before anybody is told. That is a decision
you make per monitor, not a global setting.

## Know what upcore is not

Being clear about this saves an evaluation.

- **Not an APM.** upcore answers *is it up, and was it up*. It does not trace
  requests, profile code or collect metrics from inside your application.
- **Not a log store.** Heartbeats and daily aggregates, with a retention you set.
  Nothing else is kept.
- **Not open source in the OSI sense.** It is source-available under
  [FSL-1.1-ALv2](https://github.com/upcore-app/upcore/blob/development/LICENSE.md): run
  it, self-host it, use it commercially inside your own organisation, read and
  fork and patch it — but do not offer it to others as a competing hosted
  product. Every release becomes Apache-2.0 two years after it is published.

## Pick a shape

The same application runs in two places. Everything after this page is written
for the first one unless it says otherwise.

  :::u-page-card
  ---
  icon: i-lucide-server

  ---
  #title
  Self-hosted

  #description
  One container on your own infrastructure, against a SQLite file or your
  PostgreSQL. One organization, no limits, nothing metered — and the updates,
  backups and certificates are yours.
  :::

  :::u-page-card
  ---
  icon: i-lucide-cloud
  variant: outline
  to: /cloud/overview
  ---
  #title
  upcore Cloud

  #description
  The same upcore, run for you by onesrv at
  [go.upcore.app](https://go.upcore.app/register). Free to start and upgradeable when you
  outgrow it; no database, no update and no certificate is yours to look after.
  :::
::

Which one to take is a question of who should operate it, not of what upcore can
do — see [Cloud or self-hosted](/cloud/self-hosted-or-cloud).
